# A cilia-associated epithelial alteration axis nominates ERICH3 as an entry point to PI3K engagement in lung adenocarcinoma


## Background
  LUAD adopts heterogeneous oncogenic signalling states, and bias toward a PI3K–AKT–mTOR–engaged state is not fully explained by canonical pathway lesions. Because membrane-proximal spatial organization can tune receptor-proximal signalling yet is difficult to quantify in human tumours, we used cilia-associated epithelial programs as a hypothesis-generating entry point to identify convergently perturbed candidates and evaluate their relationship with PI3K engagement.


## Methods
  We performed intersection-based multi-omic prioritization in TCGA-LUAD, evaluated dependency directionality using DepMap/CCLE expression with CRISPR dependency profiles, and tested the functional relationship between ERICH3 loss and PI3K engagement by siRNA perturbation with PI3K-inhibitor rescue. We anchored tumour context using bulk co-expression, spatial transcriptomics, and single-cell analyses with ERICH3 stratified by transcript “detection”, complemented by CellChat-based communication inference and structure/variant-informed hypothesis nomination.


## Results
  ERICH3 was prioritized through a cilia-informed screen as a convergently attenuated candidate, showed reduced protein in paired LUAD specimens (16/24, 66.7%; P = 0.014), promoter hypermethylation, and favourable survival association. An external DepMap dependency screen nominated PI3K–AKT–mTOR dependency directionality in ERICH3-low contexts (Hallmark NES = − 1.79; P = 1.04 × 10⁻4), which was subsequently evaluated functionally in LUAD cell lines.

  In LUAD cell lines, ERICH3 knockdown increased PI3K–AKT–mTOR phosphorylation and malignant phenotypes, which were attenuated by PI3K inhibition, supporting a functional relationship between ERICH3 loss and PI3K pathway engagement. Multi-scale transcriptomic analyses showed that ERICH3 transcripts were detected predominantly in a mucous epithelial compartment and non-tumour epithelium; CellChat suggested hypothesis-generating immune-interaction signals associated with ERICH3-detected mucous cells. Structural modelling and tumour-variant mapping nominated discrete membrane-proximal regions for mechanism-discriminating follow-up.


## Conclusions
  Our findings support a testable model in which attenuation of ERICH3, identified through a cilia-informed epithelial framework, is associated with a PI3K-engaged malignant state in LUAD. The present study does not establish that ERICH3 directly regulates primary-cilium formation, ciliary protein localization, or ligand-induced ciliary signalling; the precise upstream membrane-proximal and/or cilia-related route remains to be defined.


## Background
  Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), the predominant histologic subtype within non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), remains a leading contributor to cancer mortality [1] despite rapid therapeutic expansion in molecularly targeted agents and immune checkpoint blockade [2]. A major barrier to durable disease control is the coexistence and plasticity of distinct oncogenic signalling states across and within tumours [3, 4]. Among these, PI3K–AKT–mTOR signalling is a central growth and survival axis [5] that can be engaged through diverse upstream inputs, yet determinants that bias tumours toward a PI3K-engaged state beyond canonical pathway lesions remain incompletely defined [6].

  One underexplored layer is membrane-proximal spatial organization [7]. Receptor-proximal signalling depends not only on pathway components but also on where signalling assemblies form [8], shaped by compartmentalized trafficking [9], local lipid environments [10] and scaffolded complexes that regulate kinase access and efficiency [11]. Disruption of such organization could, in principle, bias pathway usage toward PI3K-axis engagement [8] but candidate regulators are difficult to surface from tumour data because they can be perturbed through heterogeneous mechanisms (expression, dosage, epigenetic silencing, or protein-altering variants) [12] and because “spatial constraint” is not captured by any single assay [13].

  Primary cilia and related apical epithelial programs provide a tractable entry point to interrogate epithelial spatial organization in the respiratory tract [14] We therefore asked which cilium-related genes show convergent evidence of recurrent disruption in LUAD and whether such disruption aligns with PI3K engagement in experimental models. Because ultrastructural readouts are difficult to standardize across heterogeneous tumour contexts, we prioritized pathway-engagement anchors and perturbation–rescue tests as primary evidence, while treating direct ciliary ultrastructure as a mechanism-discriminating follow-up [15]. Using an intersection-based prioritization strategy, coupled with dependency-resource directionality, perturbation–rescue evidence for PI3K requirement, and epithelial-context anchoring across bulk, spatial and single-cell datasets, we develop a defensible working model linking a cilia-associated alteration axis to PI3K-engaged malignant states in LUAD and nominate membrane-proximal regions for mechanistic follow-up via structural modelling and tumour-variant mapping.


## Materials and methods

### Data sources and preprocessing
  Comprehensive multi-omic data for LUAD (bulk RNA-seq, somatic SNVs, CNV, Illumina HumanMethylation450 DNA methylation, and matched clinical annotations) were obtained from TCGA [16]. Bulk RNA-seq expression values were transformed to log2(TPM + 1) [17]. Somatic SNVs were obtained from TCGA MAF files [16].

  For CNV analyses, segmented CNV profiles were used to define deletion segments and to construct gene-level deletion sets by mapping segments to gene coordinates (GenomicRanges) [18]. External datasets included GSE189357 (single-cell RNA-seq) [19], E-MTAB-13530 (spatial transcriptomics) [19], and DepMap public releases [20]. Single-cell data were re-annotated using marker-guided approaches based on canonical CellMarker genes (e.g., MUC5AC, SPDEF) [21]. Analyses were performed in R (v4.4.0) [22] using Seurat (v4) [23], clusterProfiler (v4) [21], and ggplot2 (v3.5) [24].


### Integrated genomic screening, expression profiling, and clinical association analyses of ERICH3 in LUAD
  To prioritize cilium-related genes showing convergent evidence of recurrent alteration in LUAD, we applied an intersection-based strategy integrating five evidence layers: (i) a curated cilia-related gene catalogue, (ii) transcripts downregulated in LUAD tumours relative to non-tumour controls [25], (iii) a mid-frequency gene-level deletion set derived from segmented CNV profiles, (iv) a chromosome 1 (chr1) segment-based deletion gene set, and (v) recurrent non-silent mutations (≥ 5% of cases). Differential expression was performed using DESeq2 with the design formula ~ condition (tumour versus normal), and downregulated genes were defined as log2FC < − 1 with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR < 0.05. Cilia-related genes were assembled from KEGG pathways (hsa04962, hsa05217) [26], Gene Ontology terms (GO:0005929, GO:0060271, GO:0003341) [27], and literature-defined components using org.Hs.eg.db (v3.12) and KEGGREST (v1.32.0). Overlap across layers was visualized using VennDiagram (v1.6.20).

  For CNV-based deletion sets, deletion segments were defined as segmented CNV regions with segment mean ≤ − 0.2. Segments were mapped to genes by genomic interval overlap (GenomicRanges). For each gene, deletion frequency was calculated as the proportion of samples with at least one overlapping deletion segment. Genes deleted in 10–20% of samples were defined as the mid-frequency deletion set. The chr1 deletion gene set was defined analogously as genes located on chromosome 1 with evidence of deletion segment overlap under the same threshold; this gene-level, segment-based definition was used for set intersection rather than arm-level GISTIC calls.

  Somatic mutation profiles were summarized using maftools (v2.16) [28]. For clinical relevance, Kaplan–Meier survival analyses were performed for overall survival (OS), progression-free interval (PFI), disease-specific survival (DSS), and disease-free interval (DFI) using survminer (v0.4.9). For each survival endpoint, the ERICH3 expression cutoff was determined separately using surv_cutpoint(), and patients with available endpoint-specific survival data were categorized into ERICH3-high and ERICH3-low groups accordingly. Survival differences were assessed using two-sided log-rank tests. ### DepMap functional dependency and pathway-level association analyses
  DepMap public datasets were used as an external functional-genomics resource to nominate ERICH3-associated pathway dependency directions for subsequent testing in lung cancer models. The cross-lineage analyses were used for contextual and hypothesis-generating purposes and were not intended to establish pan-cancer effects [20]. ERICH3 mRNA expression values were paired with genome-wide Chronos gene-effect profiles in matched cell lines. For each gene, Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated between ERICH3 expression and Chronos gene-effect scores across cell lines, and genes were ranked by the negative correlation coefficient (− r) to prioritize genes tending to show stronger essentiality in ERICH3-low contexts. Pre-ranked gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed using the MSigDB Hallmark collection (h.all.v2023.2.Hs.symbols.gmt) with clusterProfiler (v4), and enrichment was summarized by normalized enrichment score (NES) and associated P values as returned by the GSEA implementation [29].

  As a clinically interpretable comparator axis, lineage-stratified differences in EGFR dependency were evaluated between ERICH3-mutant and wild-type cell lines using Chronos gene-effect scores. Cell lines were annotated for ERICH3 mutation status using DepMap mutation tables and grouped by Oncotree lineage; lineages were included if they contained at least three ERICH3-mutant and five wild-type cell lines. For each lineage, mean EGFR Chronos scores were summarized in mutant and wild-type groups, and the difference was reported as Δ = (Mutant − Wild-type) on the native Chronos scale. Chronos gene-effect scores were analyzed and reported as provided by DepMap without additional transformation. These DepMap analyses are associational and were used to contextualize dependency patterns rather than establish causality.


### Experimental validation of ERICH3 function in non-small-cell lung cancer
  Patients and tissue specimens. This study was approved by the ethical committee of Tianjin Medical University General Hospital (TJMUGH, Tianjin, China). Twenty-four pairs of fresh LUAD tissues and matched non-cancerous lung tissues were collected after written informed consent was obtained from all participants. All patients had histologically confirmed LUAD and had not received chemotherapy or radiotherapy before surgery. After resection, specimens were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at − 80 °C until use.

  Cell culture and pathway inhibitor. Normal control cell lines included Beas-2B (human bronchial epithelial cells). NSCLC cell lines included A549, H1299, H1792, H1975, SPC-A-1, YTMLC-90, SK-MES-1, H661 and H460. They were obtained from the cell bank of the Tianjin Lung Cancer Institute (TJLCI, Tianjin, China) and cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA) supplemented with 10% FBS (NEWZERUM, Christchurch, New Zealand), 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin in a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO2 at 37 °C. To inhibit PI3K/Akt signaling, cells were treated with 10 μM LY294002 [30] (Selleck, TX, USA) 24 h after transfection. An equal volume of DMSO was added to control cells.

  Short interference RNA (siRNA) and transfection. Gene silencing experiments were performed using synthesized siRNA duplexes (GenePharma, Shanghai, China). Cell transfection was performed using Neofect™ siRNA transfection reagent (Neofect, Beijing, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. siERICH3−1:5′-GCUGCAUAUAAUAGCCUUATT-3′; siERICH3−2:5′-GGAAAUAUGCAGCCUCCAATT-3′; negative control siRNA: 5′-UUCUCCGAACGUGUCACGUTT-3′.

  Protein extraction and Western blotting. Western blotting was performed as previously described [31]. Briefly, tissues or cells were lysed on ice for 30 min in RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), supplemented with 1 × protease inhibitor cocktail (MCE, NJ, USA) and 1 × phosphatase inhibitor cocktail (APExBIO, TX, USA). The supernatant was collected after centrifugation at 4 °C, 12,800 rpm for 30 min. Equal amounts of protein were resolved on SDS-PAGE and transferred to an NC membrane (Pall, NY, USA). The membrane was blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 h at room temperature and incubated with primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight. Primary antibodies included β-actin (A5316, Sigma-Aldrich Co., MO, USA), ERICH3 (orb512844, Biorbyt, Cambridge, UK), phospho-Akt (Ser473) (#4060, Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA), Akt (pan) (#4691, Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA), phospho-GSK-3β (Ser9) (#5558, Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA), GSK-3β (#12,456, Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA), phospho-mTOR (Ser2448) (#5536, Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA) and mTOR (#2983, Cell Signaling Technology, MA, USA). After incubation with horseradish peroxidase-linked secondary antibodies (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China), immune complexes were detected using Pierce™ ECL Western Blotting Substrate (Thermo Fisher Scientific, MA, USA). Quantification of protein expression was performed using ImageJ software v1.8.0 (NIH, MD, USA).

  Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. CCK-8 assay was performed to evaluate cell growth ability as described previously [32]. Indicated A549 and H1299 cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 3 × 10(^3) cells/well and incubated for 4 days. Each day, 10 μL WST®−8 from the CCK-8 kit (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) was added to each well and incubated in the dark at 37 °C for 2 h. Absorbance at 450 nm was determined with a microplate reader (BioTek, VT, USA).

  Colony formation assay. Colony formation assay was performed to evaluate cell survival ability as described previously [33]. Briefly, A549 and H1299 cells were seeded into 6-well plates at a density of 1 × 10(^3) cells per well. Medium was changed every other day. Two weeks later, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min, washed with PBS twice, and stained with crystal violet staining solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) for 30 min. Cells were then washed, air-dried and photographed.

  EdU staining assay. EdU staining assay was performed to evaluate cell proliferation ability using EdU Imaging Kits (Cy3) (APExBIO, TX, USA) as described previously [31]. Transfected cells were seeded into 96-well plates and incubated for 72 h. For EdU labeling, culture medium was supplemented with 10 μM EdU and incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 2 h. Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min and permeabilized with 0.5% Triton X-100 in PBS for 15 min at room temperature. After washing, cells were incubated with EdU reaction buffer in the dark at room temperature for 30 min. Subsequently, nuclei were counterstained with Hoechst 33,342 in the dark at room temperature for 15 min. Images were captured by fluorescence microscopy (Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan) from randomly selected fields. The EdU-positive cell rate was calculated as the ratio of EdU-positive cells (Cy3 channel) to total cells (Hoechst channel) multiplied by 100%.

  Cell cycle analysis. Cell cycle analysis was determined by propidium iodide (PI) staining, as described previously [34]. Briefly, cells were fixed with 70% ice-cold ethanol and stained with nuclei staining buffer containing 0.1% Triton X-100 (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) in PBS, RNase A (200 μg/mL; Solarbio, Beijing, China), and PI (50 μg/mL; Solarbio, Beijing, China) for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark. Cell-cycle histograms were generated by flow cytometry using a NovoCyte Flow Cytometer (Agilent Technologies, CA, USA). The percentages of cells in G0/G1, S, and G2/M phases were quantified.

  Transwell assay. Transwell assay was carried out to explore cell invasion ability. Transfected cells were seeded into the upper chamber of Transwell® permeable supports with polycarbonate (PC) membrane (Corning Incorporated, NY, USA) coated with Matrigel® matrix (Corning Incorporated, NY, USA) at a density of 3 × 10(^4) cells/well in serum-free medium. Cells were allowed to invade toward the bottom chamber containing medium supplemented with 10% FBS for 24 h. Invaded cells on the lower surface were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde and stained with crystal violet staining solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China). Non-invaded cells on the upper surface were removed with cotton swabs. Invaded cells were photographed under an inverted microscope (200 × magnification) and counted in at least three fields.

  Wound healing assay. Cell migration ability was determined by wound healing assay. Briefly, transfected cells were cultured in 6-well plates to 100% confluence. A 1 mL plastic pipette tip was used to generate a wound across the center of the well. Cell debris was removed by washing with PBS, and cells were allowed to migrate in medium. Wounds were imaged at 72 h (200 × magnification), and the distance between wound edges was calculated from three positions. At least four wound areas were assessed on each plate to quantify migration.

  Unless otherwise stated, experiments were conducted with independent biological replicates and yielded consistent results across repeated experiments; representative images are shown.

  Exploratory dual-luciferase reporter assays. A549 and H1299 cells were seeded at a subconfluent density and co-transfected with 0.1 μg of a pathway-responsive firefly luciferase reporter containing GLI-, TCF-, or Smad-binding elements, 0.02 μg of a Renilla luciferase reporter as an internal transfection control, and negative-control siRNA (NC), si-ERICH3#1, or si-ERICH3#2. At 48 h after transfection, firefly and Renilla luciferase activities were measured using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). Firefly luciferase activity was normalized to the corresponding Renilla luciferase activity and then expressed relative to the NC group, which was set to 1 within each cell line.

  The assays were performed under routine culture conditions without exogenous SHH, Wnt, or BMP ligand stimulation. Serum-starvation-induced ciliogenesis was not established in this reporter system. These experiments were therefore used only as an exploratory assessment of basal GLI-, TCF-, and Smad-responsive transcriptional activity following acute ERICH3 knockdown, rather than as an assessment of ligand-induced or primary-cilium-dependent pathway responsiveness. Data were obtained from three independent biological experiments.


### Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA)
  Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) was performed to characterize ERICH3-associated co-expression patterns in TCGA-LUAD tumours [35]. A TPM expression matrix from TCGA-LUAD tumour samples (gene × sample) was used as input and transposed to the sample × gene format required by WGCNA. Co-expression modules were constructed using the blockwiseModules function in the WGCNA package with the following parameters: maxBlockSize = 5000, soft-thresholding power = 6, TOMType = “unsigned”, minModuleSize = 50, deepSplit = 2, mergeCutHeight = 0.25, and numericLabels = TRUE. Topological overlap matrices were saved (saveTOMs = TRUE; saveTOMFileBase = “TOM”). Module labels were converted to colour labels using labels2colors, and module eigengenes (MEs) were computed using moduleEigengenes.

  To characterize correlations between modules and ERICH3- or immune-associated features, a trait matrix was constructed using TPM values of ERICH3 and selected marker genes and scores: PDCD1 (PD-1), CD274 (PD-L1), CTLA4, CD8A, NCAM1, CD19, CD68, IL6, TNF and IFNG, together with ESTIMATE-derived ImmuneScore, StromalScore and ESTIMATEScore [36]. Pearson correlations between module eigengenes and traits were calculated using cor (use = “p”), and corresponding P values were computed using corPvalueStudent.

  For module prioritization, a weighted composite score was calculated for each module based on the absolute correlations with ERICH3 and immune-related traits, where ERICH3 correlations were upweighted (2 ×) and checkpoint correlations were moderately upweighted (1.5 ×). The composite score was used for module prioritization rather than statistical inference. Modules were ranked by this composite score and visualised as a module–trait correlation heatmap (pheatmap). The tan module (MEtan) was selected for main-text visualization based on its strong correlation with ERICH3, and MEtan was further examined by Pearson correlation tests against ERICH3 and PDCD1 expression and displayed as scatter plots with linear regression fits. Additional module–trait summaries are provided in the Supplementary Materials.All WGCNA results were interpreted as descriptive co-expression and correlation patterns and were not used to infer direct regulation, causal relationships, or ERICH3-dependent immune mechanisms.


### Spatial transcriptomics and single-cell RNA-seq analyses
  Public 10 × Genomics Visium NSCLC spatial transcriptomic data (E-MTAB-13530) were analysed using Seurat [23]. The dataset comprised 40 Visium Sects. (20 tumour, 16 adjacent normal, and 4 healthy normal; 88,520 spots total). Tissue category was assigned using a priori slide-level labels provided in the accompanying SDRF/metadata (sampling site: tumour, normal tissue adjacent to tumour, or healthy tissue). All quality-control–passing sections were included in quantitative comparisons across tissue categories. For figure visualization, representative QC-passing sections were selected post hoc to illustrate the typical spatial contrast between tumour and adjacent/bronchus-like regions; this selection was performed for figure clarity and did not affect analyses conducted on the full cohort. Spatial count matrices were imported using Load10X_Spatial (). Mitochondrial genes (MT-), ribosomal genes (RPS/RPL) and mitochondrial ribosomal genes (MRP) were removed; genes detected in ≤ 10 spots were excluded. Spots failing standard QC (low UMI/feature counts or high mitochondrial fraction; mt% > 10%) were removed. Data were normalized using SCTransform [37], followed by PCA (20 PCs), UMAP embedding, and graph-based clustering using Seurat’s standard workflow. All spatial visualizations were generated from the SCTransform-normalized assay unless otherwise stated. Spatial ERICH3 transcript abundance was visualized using SpatialFeaturePlot ().

  Single-cell transcriptomes from nine LUAD resections (TD1–TD9; GSE189357; 10 × Genomics v3) were processed in Seurat. Cells were filtered to retain 200–2,500 detected genes and ≤ 5% mitochondrial reads, yielding 80,490 high-quality cells. Data were log-normalized, and the 2,000 most variable genes were used for PCA and UMAP (30 PCs). Patient-level batch effects were corrected using Harmony (v1.2.0) [38]. UMAP embedding and clustering were performed on Harmony-corrected PCs. Clustering was performed using Harmony-corrected PCs (FindClusters (), resolution = 0.5), and clusters were annotated using canonical markers curated from the CellMarker database [39], together with established epithelial and immune markers. Sixteen major populations spanning epithelial, immune and stromal compartments formed the reference framework for ERICH3 transcript-detection and cell-type distribution analyses.

  Because scRNA-seq data are affected by transcript dropout and the dataset does not provide ERICH3 mutation status, downstream comparisons were performed as expression-state associations rather than genotype-defined effects. Mucous epithelial cells were stratified by ERICH3 “detection”, defined as log-normalized ERICH3 expression > 0 versus = 0. To reduce visual complexity, the main text displays summary bar plots quantifying ERICH3 detection across major cell types, whereas UMAP visualizations and additional embedding-based plots are provided in the Supplementary Materials. The spatial transcriptomic and single-cell analyses were interpreted as descriptive and contextual evidence of ERICH3 transcript distribution and expression-state associations, rather than as evidence of direct regulation or causal mechanism.


### Cell–cell communication analysis (CellChat)
  Cell–cell communication was inferred using CellChat [40] based on the processed LUAD scRNA-seq dataset (GSE189357). The integrated Seurat object was loaded from a Harmony-corrected, annotated dataset, and cell identities were defined by the metadata field cell_group. To focus on the epithelial compartment highlighted in the main analyses, mucous epithelial cells were identified and stratified by ERICH3 detection using log-normalized expression from the RNA assay (slot = "data"), defining ERICH3-detected mucous cells as ERICH3 expression > 0 and ERICH3-undetected mucous cells as ERICH3 = 0. Cells were relabelled accordingly as ERICH3_detected_Mucous and ERICH3_undetected_Mucous.

  To reduce computational burden while preserving the full ERICH3-detected sender population, all ERICH3-detected mucous cells were retained and 10,000 cells were randomly sampled from all remaining cell groups (set.seed = 42). A CellChat object was created using the log-normalized RNA expression matrix and corresponding metadata (createCellChat, group.by = "cell_group"), and CellChatDB.human was used as the ligand–receptor reference database. The standard workflow was applied, including subsetData, identifyOverExpressedGenes, and identifyOverExpressedInteractions. Communication probabilities and permutation-derived significance were computed using computeCommunProb (default nboot = 100), and interactions were filtered using filterCommunication(min.cells = 10). Pathway-level communication was inferred using computeCommunProbPathway, and overall communication networks were summarized using aggregateNet.

  Inferred directional communication patterns were summarized using subsetCommunication by restricting sources or targets to ERICH3-detected mucous cells, and network visualizations were generated using netVisual_circle and netVisual_bubble. For quantitative summaries, Σprob denotes the sum of CellChat communication probabilities (prob) across retained ligand–receptor pairs for a given sender–receiver cell-type pair (unitless). Because CellChat communication maps are inference-based and depend on transcript abundance, results were interpreted as hypothesis-generating contextual cues rather than mechanistic proof. These analyses were not used to infer direct ERICH3-dependent regulation of ligand–receptor interactions, immune-cell behaviour, or tumour-microenvironment remodelling. To reduce visual complexity, the main text presents key network summaries, whereas additional ligand–receptor tables, pathway/category breakdowns, and extended visualizations are provided in the Supplementary Materials.


### In-silico structural modelling and tumour mutation mapping
  AlphaFold2-predicted models of full-length human ERICH3 (UniProt ID: Q5RHP9) were obtained from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database [41]. The primary structural visualization in the main figures was based on the highest-ranked model, and per-residue prediction confidence was taken from the provided pLDDT scores. Structures were colored by pLDDT confidence using the bins shown in the figure legend (high confidence, pLDDT > 90; intermediate, 70–90; low, 50–70; very low, < 50).

  To highlight putative high-risk regions, residue-level pathogenicity scores (as provided with the downloaded ERICH3 structure/variant annotation resource) were mapped onto the ERICH3 sequence and visualized using the same thresholds as shown in the figure legend (> 0.564, 0.34–0.564, and < 0.34).

  Somatic ERICH3 mutations in TCGA-LUAD were retrieved from mutation annotation format (MAF) files using TCGAbiolinks and summarized with maftools. Non-silent variants (e.g., missense, nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site) were retained to compute cohort-level mutation frequency and to identify recurrent sites. Residue-level variants were mapped to the ERICH3 amino-acid coordinates for lollipop visualization and contextual interpretation relative to structural regions and annotated domains (including DUF4590, residues 395–552).

  To characterize the membrane-proximal candidate segment (residues 615–700), the ERICH3 amino-acid sequence was scanned and residues were categorized by standard physicochemical classes (acidic, basic, charged, polar) and visualized as a sequence feature track.Rank-to-rank structural convergence was assessed using inter-residue contact maps generated in UCSF ChimeraX (v1.6) [42] (Supplementary Fig. S7A–C).


### Statistical analysis
  All bioinformatic analyses were conducted in R (v4.4.0). Differential expression in TCGA-LUAD bulk RNA-seq data was performed using DESeq2, with Benjamini–Hochberg correction applied and false discovery rate (FDR) < 0.05 considered significant. Single-cell differential expression was performed in Seurat using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with BH correction (FDR < 0.05).

  Functional enrichment analyses were conducted using clusterProfiler preranked GSEA, with 10,000 permutations and q < 0.25 considered significant where applicable. Experimental data were analyzed in GraphPad Prism 8. Data are presented as mean ± SD and were compared using one-way ANOVA, with P < 0.05 considered statistically significant.


## Results

### ERICH3 is convergently attenuated in LUAD and associates with prognosis
  Given the potential for cilia-associated epithelial programs to reflect membrane-proximal spatial organization, we asked which cilium-related genes show convergent evidence of recurrent alteration in LUAD. We applied a conservative, multi-layer prioritization strategy integrating five complementary evidence layers: a curated list of ciliary genes, transcripts downregulated in LUAD, segment-based gene-level copy-number losses, a chromosome 1 (chr1) deletion gene set, and recurrent non-silent mutations. Under this intersection-based filter, ERICH3 was consistently prioritized as the top-ranked candidate (Fig. 1A).

  To validate ERICH3 attenuation at the protein level, we examined paired clinical specimens. Western blotting in 24 matched LUAD tumours and adjacent normal tissues showed reduced ERICH3 protein in 16/24 (66.7%) tumours relative to their paired controls, with a significant overall decrease across pairs (P = 0.014) (Fig. 1B). In TCGA-LUAD methylation data, promoter hypermethylation provided an additional epigenetic signal consistent with ERICH3 attenuation (FDR q < 0.05; Supplementary Fig. S1A). Additional in-vitro phenotyping readouts extending ERICH3-loss effects (CCK-8 growth, cell-cycle profiling, migration and invasion) are provided in Supplementary Fig. S1B–E.

  Finally, we assessed the clinical relevance of ERICH3 expression in LUAD outcomes. Higher ERICH3 expression was associated with improved survival endpoints, including overall survival (OS, P = 0.012), progression-free interval (PFI, P = 0.009), disease-specific survival (DSS, P = 0.019), and disease-free interval (DFI, P = 0.004) (Fig. 1C). Together, these convergent attenuation signals and clinical associations motivated us to ask whether ERICH3-low contexts exhibit directional pathway-engagement liabilities—particularly along the PI3K axis—in public functional-genomics resources and lung cancer models.


### ERICH3-low contexts nominate PI3K-axis dependency signals in public resources
  Building on the convergent attenuation and clinical associations observed in LUAD, we next asked whether ERICH3-low contexts exhibit directional pathway-level dependency signals in public functional-genomics resources and whether these signals are consistent with a PI3K-engagement direction.   Together, the public-resource analyses were used to nominate the PI3K axis for experimental testing; the principal functional conclusions of the present study are based on ERICH3 perturbation and PI3K-inhibitor rescue in lung cancer models.

### ERICH3 structural and tumour-variant mapping nominate membrane-proximal regions for mechanistic follow-up
  Having anchored ERICH3 to a spatially restricted mucous epithelial niche, we next used in-silico structural modelling and tumour-variant mapping to nominate testable membrane-proximal regions for mechanistic follow-up.   In TCGA-LUAD, 14.8% of tumours harboured non-silent ERICH3 mutations (Supplementary Fig. S7D), with recurrent hotspots R617T/S and S688Y near the DUF4590–helix interface (Fig. 6C). Together, these features provide a structural rationale for prioritizing membrane-proximal ERICH3 regions, particularly the DUF4590–helix interface and the adjacent charged segment, as testable candidates that may connect ERICH3 disruption to the PI3K-engagement phenotypes observed in dependency and perturbation–rescue assays. This plausibility layer is supportive rather than mechanistic proof and motivates targeted interface/hinge perturbation in future studies.


## Discussion
  In this study, we propose a working model in which attenuation of the cilia-associated epithelial factor ERICH3 aligns with a PI3K-engaged malignant state in LUAD. Rather than claiming a completed “ciliary gating” mechanism, we operationalize a spatial-organization perspective by linking a convergent tumour alteration axis to PI3K pathway engagement and functional requirement. Across tumours, ERICH3 shows reproducible attenuation signals with clinical association. Specifically, ERICH3 was prioritized by an intersection of five complementary alteration layers, showed reduced protein in paired LUAD specimens (16/24; P = 0.014), and higher ERICH3 expression associated with improved OS/PFI/DSS/DFI in TCGA-LUAD (Fig. 1; Supplementary Fig. S1A). In lung cancer models, ERICH3 loss increases PI3K–AKT–mTOR phosphorylation and malignant phenotypes, and these effects are attenuated by PI3K inhibition. Together, these data position ERICH3 as a tractable entry point for studying how epithelial spatial organization may bias signalling-state usage; importantly, our evidence supports pathway engagement and dependence rather than a resolved subcellular mechanism [42].

  A key conceptual question in LUAD is why tumours adopt PI3K-engaged states beyond what canonical pathway lesions explain given that clinically relevant signalling states can reflect integrated upstream inputs and context-dependent network engagement rather than a single canonical lesion alone [1]. Consistent with a context-dependent engagement model, DepMap correlation-ranked GSEA nominated PI3K–AKT–mTOR dependency signals in ERICH3-low contexts (NES = − 1.79, P = 1.04 × 10⁻4), while EGFR dependency showed lineage-dependent differences rather than a uniform effect (Fig. 2). Membrane-proximal organization offers a plausible but under-measured layer: receptor-proximal signalling output depends on where signalling assemblies form, shaped by polarized trafficking, local lipid environments and scaffold availability.

  Primary cilia and related apical epithelial programs are well positioned to influence membrane-proximal signalling through compartmentalized trafficking, phosphoinositide distribution, and spatially restricted signalling assemblies. Consistent with this biological context, class I PI3K signalling has been reported to generate PIP3 at the ciliary transition zone and regulate ciliary disassembly, providing a potential link between PI3K activity and ciliary compartmentalization [43]. ERICH3 has also been reported in cilia-related localization and functional contexts [44] These observations provided the biological rationale for our cilia-informed screening strategy.

  However, the ERICH3–primary cilium connection in the present study remains indirectly supported and should be regarded as a testable working hypothesis rather than an experimentally established mechanism. We did not directly assess primary-cilium abundance, morphology, length, or the ciliary localization of signalling proteins. Moreover, the GLI-, TCF-, and Smad-responsive reporter assays were conducted under basal conditions without validated ciliogenesis or pathway-specific ligand stimulation; accordingly, these assays neither establish nor exclude an effect of ERICH3 on ligand-induced, primary-cilium-dependent SHH, Wnt, or BMP signalling. By contrast, acute ERICH3 knockdown reproducibly increased PI3K–AKT–mTOR phosphorylation and malignant phenotypes in A549 and H1299 cells, and these effects were attenuated by PI3K inhibition (Figs. 3–4; Supplementary Figs. S1–S2). Thus, the current functional evidence supports ERICH3-associated PI3K pathway engagement and dependence, while the precise ciliary and/or broader membrane-proximal route remains unresolved.

  The conceptual contribution of this study is therefore the proposal of a testable model linking ERICH3 attenuation, a cilia-associated epithelial alteration state, and PI3K pathway engagement, while direct validation of the upstream ciliary route remains an important objective for future work.

  Within this conservative framing, multi-scale transcriptomic analyses provide descriptive anatomical and cellular context for ERICH3 expression [45]. In bulk LUAD, co-expression analysis identified an ERICH3-associated expression pattern with little correlation with PDCD1 in this cohort (Fig. 5A–B). This descriptive observation does not establish independence from immune infiltration or direct regulation of immune signalling by ERICH3.Spatial and single-cell analyses showed that ERICH3 transcripts were detected predominantly in a mucous epithelial compartment and were relatively preserved in non-tumour regions, providing descriptive anatomical and cellular context for the bulk signal [46] (Fig. 5C–G). Network parameterization, sample clustering QC, and the global module–immune trait correlation landscape are provided in Supplementary Fig. S3. Whole-section spatial transcriptomic analyses further illustrate how tissue-scale context can inform the interpretation of bulk tumour signals and apparent immune mixtures.In scRNA-seq, we explicitly treat comparisons as expression-state associations (ERICH3-detected versus undetected mucous epithelial cells), reflecting dropout and the absence of genotype information; these contrasts should not be interpreted as mutation-defined effects [47]. Finally, CellChat provided hypothesis-generating, inference-based context, with higher inferred outgoing communication probabilities from ERICH3-detected mucous cells toward immune populations, most prominently CD8⁺ T cells; these patterns require orthogonal validation [48]. Directional ligand–receptor mapping and pathway/category summaries of these inferred patterns are shown in Supplementary Figs. S5–S6. In line with current best practice, we treat inferred cell–cell communication as hypothesis-generating rather than definitive evidence of in situ ligand–receptor function, motivating orthogonal validation [49].

  Structural modelling and tumour-variant mapping provide a hypothesis-generating plausibility layer for nominating testable structure–function relationships and do not constitute mechanistic proof [50]. In TCGA-LUAD, ERICH3 harboured non-silent mutations in ~ 14.8% of tumours, with recurrent hotspots (R617T/S and S688Y) near the DUF4590–helix interface and an adjacent charged/basic segment (~ 615–700) nominating testable membrane-proximal regions (Fig. 6; Supplementary Fig. S7). A high-confidence DUF4590 core, recurrent variants concentrated near the DUF4590–helix interface, and an adjacent charged/basic stretch compatible with a candidate membrane-proximal interaction region together nominate discrete regions for targeted perturbation in future experiments. In this framing, structure-guided perturbations (e.g., interface-disrupting or charge-neutralizing substitutions) provide a falsifiable route to distinguish “membrane-proximal organization” effects from generic loss-of-function consequences [51].

  Taken together, our data are consistent with a continuum in which ERICH3-intact states show more restrained PI3K engagement, whereas ERICH3 attenuation aligns with increased PI3K-associated signalling and PI3K-dependent phenotypes [1]. The concordance across independent siRNAs, multiple cellular backgrounds, and pharmacologic rescue argues against reagent-specific artefacts [52]. In contrast, DepMap directionality and lineage-dependent EGFR effects should be interpreted as context-dependent dependency signals that inform hypothesis generation, rather than as predictors of monotherapy benefit.


## Limitations and future directions
  Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the functional experiments relied primarily on transient ERICH3 knockdown and pharmacological PI3K inhibition. Stable knockout or knock-in models, ERICH3 overexpression and genetic rescue experiments, and in vivo validation were not performed. These approaches will be required to establish the specificity, durability, and physiological relevance of the phenotypes observed following acute ERICH3 knockdown. Because LY294002 may exert off-target effects, the sensitivity of these phenotypes to PI3K-pathway inhibition should be confirmed using additional selective PI3K inhibitors and/or genetic inhibition of PI3K pathway components.

  Second, the proposed relationship between ERICH3 and primary-cilium biology remains indirect. We did not directly quantify cilium frequency, morphology, or length, nor did we assess the ciliary localization of pathway components. A validated serum-starvation-induced ciliogenesis model was not established in the present study, and the reporter assays were not performed under pathway-specific ligand stimulation. Therefore, the current data do not establish whether ERICH3 directly regulates primary-cilium formation or ligand-induced SHH, Wnt, or BMP signalling. Future studies using robust ciliogenesis-competent epithelial models, ciliary imaging, ligand stimulation, ERICH3 overexpression or genetic rescue, and stable genetic or in vivo models will be needed to distinguish a cilium-specific mechanism from broader membrane-proximal signalling organization.

  Third, the bulk co-expression, spatial transcriptomic, single-cell, WGCNA, and CellChat analyses are descriptive and hypothesis-generating. ERICH3 detection in scRNA-seq is affected by transcript dropout, and inferred ligand–receptor communication does not demonstrate direct regulation of immune signalling or tumour-microenvironment remodelling. These findings require orthogonal experimental validation. In addition, although ERICH3 was predominantly detected in a mucous epithelial compartment, MUC4, MUC5-family members, MUC16, and broader mucin-remodelling programs were not systematically evaluated. A potential relationship between ERICH3 and mucin biology therefore remains an important direction for future investigation rather than a conclusion of the present study.


## Conclusions
  ERICH3 is recurrently attenuated in LUAD and is associated with clinical outcome. Across public dependency data and perturbation–rescue experiments, ERICH3 loss aligns with a PI3K-engaged state characterized by increased PI3K–AKT–mTOR phosphorylation and PI3K-dependent malignant phenotypes in lung cancer models. Multi-scale transcriptomic analyses show that ERICH3 transcripts are detected predominantly in a mucous epithelial compartment, while structural and tumour-variant mapping nominate discrete membrane-proximal regions for mechanism-focused follow-up. Together, these findings support a testable working model in which ERICH3 attenuation is associated with a cilia-associated epithelial alteration state and PI3K engagement in LUAD. The present study does not establish that ERICH3 directly regulates primary-cilium formation, ciliary protein localization, or ligand-induced ciliary signalling; targeted studies of ciliogenesis, membrane-proximal signalling organization, genetic rescue, and in vivo function will be required to define the upstream mechanism.

Fusarium fungi cause economically important diseases of cereal crops, including Fusarium head blight, ear blight, and root rot. Central to these interactions are secreted effector proteins that manipulate host biology to promote infection. In turn, plants have evolved to recognize effector activity to activate immune responses and restrict disease. Despite their importance, the diversity, conservation, and evolution of Fusarium effectors remain poorly understood. Here, we present the Fusarium sambucinum Species complex Effector Compendium (FUSEC), a sequence-defined pan-effectome constructed from 188 high-quality genomes representing 27 species. FUSEC comprises 4,057 sequence-defined effector families, including a conserved soft-core repertoire of 235 families present in at least 95% of isolates. Structure-guided comparisons reorganized these conserved families into 184 structure-defined groups, revealing relationships that extend beyond sequence similarity and identifying similarities to numerous experimentally characterized fungal (a)virulence factors. Despite their broad conservation, soft-core families exhibited substantial within-family sequence diversity, and analyses of diversifying selection identified 44 candidate families undergoing adaptive evolution. Functional screening revealed that the majority of these candidates altered wheat immune responses, and CRISPR-Cas9-mediated gene deletions in Fusarium graminearum demonstrated that multiple conserved effectors contribute to cultivar-dependent Fusarium head blight severity.

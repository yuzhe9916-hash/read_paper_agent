Precision engineering of CRISPR/Cas9 components has advanced genome editing toward therapeutic applications. Fully chemically modified guide RNAs (gRNAs) have the potential to improve stability and broaden therapeutic delivery strategies. However, extensive chemical modification of CRISPR guides can compromise genome editing activity by disrupting gRNA-Cas9 interactions. Here, we employed an iterative, structure-guided optimization strategy to systematically evaluate chemical modifications throughout SpyCas9 gRNAs. Extending beyond commonly used nucleotide modifications, we incorporated 2'-amino-RNA, 4'-thio-RNA, and extended nucleic acid (exNA) to generate gRNAs in which 90%-100% of nucleotides are sugar- or backbone-modified. Although the performance of individual modification patterns varied among target sequences, we established a repertoire of guides that support robust genome editing in vitro and in vivo. Collectively, these findings provide a systematic framework for rational engineering of heavily and fully modified gRNAs using diverse nucleotide chemistries for nonviral CRISPR genome editing.

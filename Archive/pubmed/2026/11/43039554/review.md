## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a well-structured and ambitious framing for a review article on polysaccharide-based nanoparticles in precision medicine. The conceptual organization around complementary fabrication paradigms (bottom-up vs. top-down) and the explicit link from molecular structure to therapeutic function is commendable and potentially valuable to the biomaterials community. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient evidence to evaluate the scholarly depth, critical analysis, or novelty of the review relative to existing literature. The scope is broad, and the abstract does not convey how the authors differentiate their framework from prior reviews in this rapidly expanding field. The mention of protein-derived systems as a secondary element is appropriate but underdeveloped in the abstract. Overall, the framing is promising, but the case for a genuinely novel contribution cannot be established from the abstract alone.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in biomaterials science, drug delivery, nanomedicine, and carbohydrate chemistry would find this review relevant. Specifically, groups working on polysaccharide-based nanocarriers, CRISPR delivery systems, and immuno-oncology nanotherapeutics would benefit from a consolidated framework linking molecular design to in vivo performance. The emphasis on scalable manufacturing and translational constraints also targets an audience interested in clinical translation of nanomedicines.

- **Major strengths** 
  1. The conceptual integration of bottom-up assembly and top-down deconstruction as complementary paradigms is a useful organizing principle that may help unify disparate literature.
  2. The explicit chain from molecular structure to assembly behavior to biological transport to therapeutic function provides a logical scaffold for the review.
  3. The focus on four major polysaccharides (cellulose, chitosan, alginate, hyaluronic acid) with clear biomedical relevance gives the review a defined scope.
  4. The inclusion of CRISPR-Cas9 delivery and immunotherapy as application areas addresses high-impact, current research directions.

Glutathione (GSH) and Hg(2+) both possess skin-lightening effects, but GSH is a beneficial component while Hg(2+) poses significant health risks to humans. Accurate quantification of both GSH and Hg(2+) is crucial for distinguishing the quality of commercial skin lightening cosmetics. In this study, the In(2)S(3)-Ti(3)C(2) photoelectric material was synthesized and modified onto the surface of FTO. By incorporating T-rich Hg(2+) aptamer DNA with ferrocene-modified termini (Apt-Fc) onto the material surface, a photoelectrochemical (PEC) aptamer sensor was developed for the sequential detection of Hg(2+) and glutathione (GSH) using the same sensing platform. In the absence of Hg(2+), Fc acts as an electron acceptor to competitively capture photogenerated electrons from In(2)S(3)-Ti(3)C(2), reducing the number of electrons transferred to the electrode and resulting in a decreased photocurrent. Upon Hg(2+) addition, the formed T-Hg(2+)-T structure brings Fc into closer proximity to the photoelectrode surface, where the steric hindrance effect of the T-Hg(2+)-T duplex and the competitive electron-capturing effect of Fc synergistically lead to a further decreased photocurrent, enabling sensitive Hg(2+) detection. On the other hand, GSH reduces MnO(2) to Mn(2+), which accelerates the CRISPR/Cas12a cleavage activity to randomly cleave the T-Hg(2+)-T chain; the resultant enhanced photocurrent enables GSH detection. The detection limits are 8.47 x 10(-12) mol L(-1) and 4.83 x 10(-11) mol L(-1) for Hg(2+) and GSH, respectively. The sensor has been successfully applied to the sequential detection of illegally added Hg(2+) and the endogenous antioxidant GSH in commercial skin lightening cosmetics.

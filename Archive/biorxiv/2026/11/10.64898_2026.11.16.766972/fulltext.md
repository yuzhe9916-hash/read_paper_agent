The CRISPR/Cas9 system has emerged as a revolutionary tool with diverse applications in genome engineering; however, the role of nanoscale hydrogen bonds between single nucleotides in precision editing remains unknown. Here we systematically compared A-rich and G-rich PAM-recognizing Cas9 editors through comprehensive analyses of ~37,000 targeting sites with about half million libraries of ~0.36 billion types of editing outcomes. We showed that the A-rich Cas9 editors exhibit stronger editing outcome predictability in comparison with the conventional Cas9 editor of the G-rich PAM. In addition, the A-rich Cas9 editors induce a higher frequency of staggered cleavages, resulting in predictable editing with donor-independent templated insertions. Moreover, we identified distinct DNA sequence motifs for predictable insertions with the A-rich Cas9 editors. In particular, we revealed single nucleotides at the -4 position as MMEJ microhomologies in precision engineering via two or three hydrogen bonds with A-rich or G-rich PAM, respectively. Furthermore, we found that PolQ plays a key role in enhanced random insertions. Finally, we developed a discrete-space diffusion model CRIfuser of deep learning for predicting editing outcomes with staggered cleavage. Together, these findings establish PAM-dependent cleavage geometry as a tunable determinant of Cas9 editing outcomes and provide a framework for predictive and precision genome engineering.
